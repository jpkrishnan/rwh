## Business Overview

Ford Motor Company designs, manufactures, and sells vehicles and related parts under the Ford and Lincoln brands. Founded in Dearborn, Michigan in 1903 by Henry Ford, the company pioneered mass-market automobile production and has operated through multiple industrial cycles to remain one of the most recognized OEM franchises globally.

Under the Ford+ plan introduced in 2022, Ford reorganized into three customer-facing business units: **Ford Pro** (commercial and fleet vehicles), **Ford Blue** (ICE and hybrid consumer vehicles), and **Ford Model e** (battery-electric vehicles). Ford Credit (Ford Motor Credit Company), a wholly-owned subsidiary providing retail financing, leasing, and fleet lending, is managed separately but consolidated into reported financials — it carries ~$130B in managed receivables and contributed $2.6B in EBT in FY2025. Ford Credit is effectively an embedded financial services business that provides earnings stability but adds substantial leverage to the consolidated balance sheet.

The company employs ~168,000 people and generated $187.3B in revenue in FY2025 with ~$57B market cap.

## §3 — Competitive Moat & Landscape

### Moat Assessment: **Narrow**

Ford's moat is narrow overall and segment-dependent. Ford Pro has the strongest moat characteristics; Ford Blue's moat rests on brand loyalty within trucks and is more fragile; Model e has no moat.

**Ford Pro moat sources (strongest)**:
1. **Fleet switching costs**: Large commercial operators (municipal utilities, construction, delivery companies) standardize on Transit and F-Series work trucks; crew training, parts inventory, authorized upfitter networks, and dealer service coverage create meaningful switching friction
2. **Software ecosystem lock-in**: Ford Pro Intelligence telematics (GPS/diagnostics), Pro Power Onboard (on-board power gen for job sites), FinSimple fleet financing, and Pro Trailer Backup Assist — 879K paid subscriptions growing 30% YoY; high-retention SaaS-like revenue
3. **Upfitter and dealer network**: ~2,700 Ford US dealers with commercial specialists; nation-wide upfitter partnerships for shelving, lighting, and trade-specific buildouts — a multi-decade infrastructure asset
4. **Commercial market scale**: ~40% US commercial vehicle market share; no competitor within 20 share points

**Ford Blue moat sources (moderate)**:
1. **F-Series contractor loyalty**: Multigenerational brand loyalty among contractors, farmers, and tradespeople; F-150 is the most-bought vehicle by US small businesses
2. **Off-road premium positioning**: Bronco's heritage off-road brand; Raptor/Tremor trims commanding $15–20K premium; off-road trims now 25% of F-150 US sales — hard to replicate quickly
3. **Lincoln**: Modest brand equity in North American luxury

**Moat vulnerabilities**:
- Commercial EV disruption: Amazon has contracted 100K Rivian EDV delivery vans; DHL piloting BYD commercial vans in US (blocked by tariffs currently); long-term fleet electrification could shift loyalty
- F-Lightning failure shows ICE-EV transition difficulty: the franchise that sustains the moat (F-Series loyalty) is not easy to transfer to EVs

|-----------|---------|------|--------------------|
| **General Motors (GM)** | Full-size trucks (Silverado, Sierra), GM Fleet | Narrow | Direct F-Series competition; Chevy/GMC commercial variants; GM down 3% YTD 2026 vs. Ford +13% — less momentum |

**Ford's stated competitive differentiator**: "83% domestic assembly" as tariff shield; software ecosystem as commercial stickiness; powertrain-choice flexibility (hybrid/EREV/BEV by customer need).

## §5 — Strategic Growth Initiatives

### 1. Ford Pro Software & Services Platform (highest conviction)

Ford Pro Intelligence platform — combining fleet management, telematics (GPS/diagnostics), Pro Power Onboard, EV charging management, FinSimple fleet financing, and authorized upfit coordination — generated **$15B in software/physical services revenue in FY2025** growing **~8% annually through 2030 target** (implied ~$22B by 2030). Paid software subscriptions reached **879K units** in Q1 2026, +30% YoY. The economics are attractive: software/services carry significantly higher margins than vehicle sales. As fleet operators embed Ford's digital tools, switching costs compound — this is a moat-building initiative within an otherwise narrow-moat company.

### 2. Hybrid/Electrified Vehicle Expansion (near-term profitable)

Hybrids are Ford's most immediately profitable EV-adjacent strategy. The Maverick Hybrid is America's best-selling hybrid truck (compact, affordable, ~40 MPG city). The F-150 Hybrid is the #1 full-size hybrid pickup. These products require no charging infrastructure investment from buyers, have stronger margins than BEVs, and satisfy fleet operators' range requirements. Ford plans to extend hybrid powertrains across its SUV/truck lineup as a bridge technology. Record electrified vehicle volume in H1 2025 (156K+ units) demonstrates commercial viability.

### 3. UEV Platform — Affordable BEV (2027, Louisville)

Ford's most important upcoming product. The **Universal Electric Vehicle (UEV) platform** launches from Ford's Louisville Assembly Complex in 2027, targeting sub-$40,000 price points — the threshold where EV purchase economics reach parity with ICE for many buyers. This is a ground-up cost architecture designed to be competitive with Tesla Model Y, Korean OEMs, and eventually Chinese OEMs. Farley's vision: "We need to win at sub-$40K." This is the pivot from the premium (and money-losing) Lightning to mass-market EV.

### 4. Extended Range Electric Vehicle (EREV) Strategy

EREVs pair an electric motor with a small gas engine as a generator — delivering EV-like driving while eliminating range anxiety. Ford views EREV as the optimal architecture for commercial and truck buyers who need dependable range for job sites or towing. This is a hedge against pure-BEV market uncertainty, qualified for EV tax credits, and avoids the charging infrastructure dependency that has limited Lightning adoption.

### 5. Cost Reduction / Quality Improvement

Ford targets **$1B/yr in material and warranty cost improvements** — a direct path to restoring gross margins from FY2025's depressed 6.8% toward the historical 14–15% range. Ford set industry records for recall volume in 2025. Each 1% improvement in warranty cost = ~$1.9B of gross profit recovery. This is the single most impactful near-term lever on profitability.

### EV Transition: Reality vs. Narrative

- US BEV penetration: ~8–9% of new vehicle sales (FY2025); growth rate below initial OEM forecasts
- Hybrid/PHEV growing faster than pure BEV as consumers accept electrification with range safety net
- Charging infrastructure gap persists; range anxiety remains a barrier for commercial fleet buyers
