## Key Facts

- **Law firm**: Hagens Berman Sobol Shapiro LLP
- **Nature**: New, materially expanded complaint distinct from earlier round of class action solicitations
- **Defendants**: Super Micro Computer, Inc. and senior executives
- **Class period**: February 2, 2024 – March 19, 2026
- **Lead plaintiff deadline**: May 26, 2026 (unchanged from prior solicitations)

## Two-Phase Deception Theory (New in This Complaint)

The expanded complaint alleges defendants conducted a two-phase deception:

1. **Phase 1 (pre-investigation)**: Public assurances of export-control compliance while the alleged smuggling scheme (Wally Liaw + associates) was ongoing
2. **Phase 2 (post-E&Y resignation)**: After the E&Y resignation (Oct 2024) and internal investigation, defendants allegedly provided only partial corrective disclosures — disclosing the accounting remediation while concealing the full scope of the export-control fraud that later resulted in the March 19, 2026 DOJ indictment

## Additional Corrective Disclosures Alleged

Hagens Berman identifies specific additional corrective disclosures beyond the DOJ announcement itself — strengthening the argument that the class period (Feb 2024–Mar 2026) captures a broader fraud arc, not just the single-session March 20, 2026 price drop.

## Investment Relevance

- The expanded theory broadens defendant list (senior executives, not just co-founder) and extends the class period backward to February 2024
- Broader class period = larger potential damage class = higher settlement exposure
- The "two-phase" scienter theory makes it harder for defendants to argue they lacked knowledge of the fraud — strengthens plaintiff's case
- Settlement probability remains high (SMCI's 2020 SEC settlement of $17.5M sets precedent); amount now potentially higher with expanded class
- *Not yet priced into the stock at $31.04*
